00|Meet H T T Scheduling. A full stack platform for scheduling student employees across campus, built on Next.js, a Go A P I, and PostgreSQL. In the next few minutes we'll follow the whole story, from a brand new account all the way to a fully staffed week. Let's take the tour.
01|New here? The login page doubles as self service registration. Signing up takes an email and a strong password, and the account is created on the spot. No waiting on an administrator.
02|Everything starts with a secure sign in. Users log in with their email or their university I D, and signing in from a new device triggers a four digit verification code. George lands on his week at a glance. Sixteen of his twenty weekly hours are already scheduled, and two pending requests sit in his queue.
03|Open shifts drop into George's work queue, and shifts he can't fit under his twenty hour cap don't even show up. This two hour block fits, so one click picks it up, and his calendar updates to eighteen of twenty hours. When life happens, requesting to miss a shift takes two clicks: pick a reason, and submit. Need a second one for another day? Same two clicks. Changed your mind about one? Cancel it right from the list, and the status flips on the spot. Preferred days and times tell managers when George is free. And the calendar view lays out his whole week at a glance.
04|Staff workers get the same self service view, with rules of their own. Alex is full time: forty hours a week of regular scheduling, overtime beyond that with approval, and a hard cap of sixty shown right on his dashboard. He picks an eight hour dining shift with one click, and his week updates to sixteen hours. Dana is part time and hourly, working to a limit her manager sets; she picks a shift the same way, landing at eighteen of her cap. Same view, same one click workflow, but every worker type has its own rules enforced automatically. And when either of them needs time off, the request flows to their manager just like a student's does. One platform, every worker type.
05|Admins run the system itself. Departments, teams, and jobs define the organization. Access control is where accounts live. Search anyone, page through the directory, and click any column header to sort it. Creating a user takes an email, a university I D, and a one click generated temporary password. A second pass fills in the rest: names, address, phone, and preferred contact. This is also where role based access control lives. Permissions climb a simple ladder: student, staff, manager, admin, and a user can hold any combination. Managers all share one rank. What differs is their scope. A department scoped manager sees every team under their department, while a team scoped manager sees only their own team. Admins are global, and only they can delete accounts or change roles. And every screen in the app is role gated.
06|An admin created account finishes onboarding on its own. The first sign in with the temporary password forces a change, and since the profile was already filled in, the new student lands straight on their dashboard, ready to work. No step skipped, and no credential ever shared.
07|Now the department manager. The org chart shows her scope at a glance: the Jester department, every team under it, and who runs each one, with a You badge wherever she's responsible. Her workers list shows each person's type and hour rules, and the job requirements section lays out coverage for every team. Adding a shift to the work queue takes seconds: pick a team, a date, and a time. Time off requests wait right here. Approving one returns the shift to the queue, for someone else to pick up. And her coverage calendar shows the whole week, color coded by worker, with a dedicated lane for open slots. Filter workers to focus, flip to next week, and the new Monday slot is already sitting in the open lane, ready for next week's plan.
08|Sam manages just the dining team. Same manager rank, narrower scope: only dining's queue, only dining's requests. Denying one leaves the shift in place. On the coverage calendar, open slots invite assignment. Click one and pick a worker; weekly hour caps are enforced right in the picker. Assign just the block you need, and the rest of the slot stays open. Changed plans? Unassign it with one click, or hand the slot straight to someone else. Priya takes the morning block, the afternoon stays open for whoever can cover it, and nobody had to make a phone call. Every change lands in the audit trail.
09|Managers get a scoped audit report: request, shift, and job activity from the last fifteen days, filtered to what they manage. Filter by activity type, page back through the record, or export the whole report to C S V for the books. A department manager sees their whole department; a team scoped manager sees only their team. Every entry is attributed and append only.
10|Role based dashboards, self serve signup, shift picking, time off workflows, hour caps with overtime approval, calendar assignment, and scoped audit trails. H T T Scheduling keeps your campus in sync. Thanks for watching.
